You can rewrite a component's internals on a Tuesday afternoon and nobody will
notice. Rename one prop and you will hear about it for a year.

This asymmetry is the whole job. The implementation is private and cheap to
change; the API is public and expensive. Yet most of the review effort in a
design system goes into the implementation, because that is the part that looks
like engineering.

## Variants are a vocabulary, not a switch

The instinct with a `variant` prop is to make it permissive — accept a string,
add a case for each new design. Six months later there are eleven variants, four
of which are used once, and the component has become a small framework nobody
chose to adopt.

A closed union of three or four variants forces the argument to happen at the
point where it is cheap: someone asks for a fifth, and the team has to decide
whether it is genuinely a new kind of thing or a one-off that belongs in the
consuming app. Usually it is the latter, and usually the person asking already
suspected that.

## Escape hatches are fine, but name them

Every system needs a way out — a `className`, a slot, something. The mistake is
pretending otherwise, which just moves the escape hatch somewhere less visible,
like a global stylesheet override targeting an internal class name.

Give it a name, document what it voids, and it stops being a workaround and
starts being a supported case with known limits.
