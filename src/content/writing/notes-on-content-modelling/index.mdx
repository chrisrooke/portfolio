The temptation, every time, is to let a layout decide a schema. You need two
things side by side on a page, so you put them in one bucket. Six months later
the bucket has nine optional fields and every consumer starts with a `switch`.

## Storage is not display

A collection should be shaped like the thing it stores. If two collections need
to appear together, that is a *view* concern, and views are cheap to build:

- Normalise the two shapes into a common item type
- Sort the combined list
- Switch on a discriminant when rendering

What you get back for the small amount of glue is two honest schemas instead of
one dishonest one.

## Validation earns its keep quietly

The value of a build-time schema isn't the types. It's that a typo in a tag
becomes a failed build naming the file, rather than a blank space that nobody
notices for a month.
