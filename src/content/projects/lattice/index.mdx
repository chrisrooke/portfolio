Lattice began as a colour palette in a spreadsheet and ended as the only
sanctioned way to build a screen at the company. The interesting part was not
the components — it was the eighteen months of negotiation that made the
components possible.

## The problem

Four product teams, three of which had forked the same button component and
diverged. Nothing was broken, exactly. But every new hire spent their first
fortnight learning which of the four codebases they were allowed to copy from,
and accessibility fixes landed in one place and nowhere else.

## What I did

I led the system from the initial audit through to adoption across all four
teams. In practice that meant roughly a third design, a third engineering, and a
third convincing people — the last of which turned out to be the constraint.

The technical shape was deliberately unambitious: tokens generated from a single
source, a component library small enough that one person could hold it in their
head, and documentation built around the twenty-second lookup rather than the
guided tour.

## Where it got to

Eleven components, one palette, and a contribution process that four teams
actually used. The button forks were retired in the third quarter, which was six
months later than planned and about a year earlier than I had privately feared.
